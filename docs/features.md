# Library and player

## Your library

- **Movies and TV**: libraries, search, rich detail pages, resume and Next Up, next and previous episode, skip intro, recap, and credits, an Up Next prompt that rolls into the next episode during the credits, chapters, and trickplay thumbnails as you scrub.
- **Search**: one search across the server finds movies, shows, episodes, collections, albums, artists, songs and people. Chips narrow it to **Movies**, **Shows**, **Episodes**, **Music** or **People**. A person opens their filmography, an artist their albums, and a song its album.
- **Inside a library**: a search box for that library, **Filter** (Unwatched, Favorites, and a genre), **Sort** (Name, Date Added, Release Date, Rating or Random, then **Ascending** or **Descending**), **List View** or **Grid View**, and **Select** to pick several titles at once. On a phone or in a narrow window, the view and **Select** are under **⋮**. Pull down, or press **F5** or **Ctrl+R** on a computer, to load the library again. When a search or filter leaves nothing, the page says **Nothing matches** and offers **Clear Filters**.
- **Browse by anything**: genres, studios, artists, and a page for every person, so a cast member's other work is one tap away. Trailers play in the app rather than sending you to a browser.
- **Extras**: a title's bonus material (behind the scenes, deleted scenes, interviews, featurettes, and trailer files held on your server) appears as its own row on the detail page, and plays in the normal player.
- **Three lists, each for one thing**: **Favorites** for what you love, the **Watchlist** for what you mean to watch, and **Playlists** for what you want played in order. Each has one page, reached from the side menu or the shortcuts at the top of **Libraries**. A title's page has a button for Favorites and for the Watchlist, and its context menu (long-press, right-click, or **⋮**) has both plus **Add to Playlist**. Each says what it did ("Added to Watchlist").
- **Favorites**: anything you love: movies, shows, episodes, collections, videos, albums, artists, songs, playlists and people. It's Jellyfin's own Favorites, so it's the same list in every Jellyfin app. Chips across the top narrow it to one kind; only kinds you have favorited get a chip.
- **Watchlist**: movies and shows you mean to watch, newest first. It takes movies and shows only: on an episode, the button reads **Add Show to Watchlist** and puts the show on. A title stays until you take it off, even after you've watched it. The [Movie Night Wheel](#your-library) starts from it. It's kept on your server as a playlist named "Fathom Watchlist", so it follows you to every device you sign in on. A Jellyfin playlist can't hold a show as itself, so a show is kept as one of its episodes, and the Watchlist shows it as the show. Jellyfin's web app shows it as a playlist: don't rename or delete it there, or Fathom starts a new, empty Watchlist.
- **Playlists**: your Jellyfin playlists, in the order you set (drag to reorder, or **Move Up** and **Move Down** on a TV). One can mix movies, episodes and songs. Create one on the **Playlists** page or with **Add to Playlist** in any title's context menu; **Rename Playlist** and **Delete Playlist** are under **⋮** on its page. Jellyfin lists a Playlists folder among your libraries; Fathom leaves it out of **Libraries**, since **Playlists** has a place of its own, and any playlist, wherever you find it (search, Favorites), opens as its list of items. **Play** starts at the top and goes through the list, **Shuffle** plays it in a random order, and tapping an item plays on from there. Movies and episodes follow one another in the player, where **Previous**, **Next** and the Up Next prompt step through the playlist, not the show; turn off **Autoplay Next** (**Settings → Player**) to stop after each. Songs play as a music queue. A list that mixes them plays in its own order all the same: the music player takes the songs, and the video player opens for the movie or episode after them (while Fathom is on screen; music playing in the background doesn't start a video by itself). An item added twice shows, and plays, twice, and each copy can be moved or removed by itself.
- **Continue Watching that follows what you watch**: one card per show, whether you stopped part-way through an episode or finished one and the next is waiting, ordered by what you actually watched most recently. A half-watched episode you abandoned long ago gives way to where you really are in the show, and a new episode of a show you were caught up on goes near the front the day it arrives.
- **Tidy up Continue Watching**: **Remove from Continue Watching** in a card's context menu takes that show or film off the row until you watch it again. If it had a resume point, that is cleared on the server too, so Jellyfin's other apps agree. Nothing is marked watched.
- **Home banner**: the banner at the top of Home mixes a few things you are in the middle of with what was added recently.
- **What's downloading and what's coming**: with [Sonarr, Radarr or Lidarr](#sonarr-radarr-and-lidarr) connected, Home gains two rows, **Downloading Now** and **Coming This Week**.
- **Movie Night Wheel**: when nobody can decide, spin for it. Open it from the **Watchlist** page, or from a SyncPlay session so a group can decide together. It starts with everything on your Watchlist; add other movies and shows with **Add from Your Library**, and untick any you'd rather skip tonight. Narrow it with **Unwatched**, **Under 2 Hours** or a genre, then pick a mode: **Last One Standing** knocks a title out on every spin, **Single Spin** takes the first result, and **Best of 3** wants a title to win twice. From the winner, **Play** starts it (a show starts at its Next Up episode, or the first one), and you can put it on or take it off the Watchlist. The mode and the titles you untick are remembered; the filters start fresh each time.
- **Music**: albums, a play queue, now playing (with a mini player along the bottom while you browse, and **Stop** on it to end the music, or YouTube audio, and put it away), shuffle and repeat, synced lyrics with an online fallback, and scrobbling. The now-playing screen flips between artwork and lyrics.
- **Live TV and DVR**: a channel list, an EPG guide, recording with series rules, and tuner and guide-provider setup.

## The player

The same player drives Jellyfin and [YouTube](youtube.md): one control bar, one seek bar (with chapter and skip markers), the same speed control, track pickers, and keyboard shortcuts, so nothing feels bolted on.

- **mpv-grade playback** through media_kit and libmpv: direct play with a transcode fallback, hardware decoding, and proper subtitle and audio track selection.
- **Click or tap the picture**: a click plays or pauses. On a touchscreen, a tap first brings the controls up, and a tap with them showing plays or pauses. Double-tap the left or right side to skip back or forward, and the middle for fullscreen.
- **Picture in picture**: shrink a video into a floating mini player and keep browsing, or pop it out to a resizable, always-on-top window. The mini player button is there in fullscreen too, and leaves fullscreen first. On Android phones and tablets, leaving Fathom while a Jellyfin or YouTube video is open (going home, or switching apps from recents or the taskbar) floats it into the system's picture-in-picture window, with its own back, Play/Pause, and forward buttons. Expanding the window brings back the player as you left it, in or out of fullscreen. A video docked in the mini player doesn't float when you leave. If nothing floats, check that picture-in-picture is allowed for Fathom in Android's settings (Apps > Fathom, or Special app access > Picture-in-picture).
- **Up Next**: for an episode that has a next one (or a video with one after it in a playlist), an Up Next card or compact Netflix-style pill appears during the credits and rolls into the next episode. Pick the style, and how long it waits before auto-skipping (the whole credits or a short countdown), under **Settings → Player**, or turn **Autoplay Next** off and use its Play Now button.
- **Audio passthrough (desktop)**: bitstream Dolby Digital, DTS, and Dolby Atmos straight to an AV receiver instead of decoding to stereo.
- **Tune it to taste**: video fit, playback speed, a control bar you can style (glass, dark, or plain), remappable keyboard shortcuts, and scrub-preview thumbnails.
- **Foldable phones**: half-fold the phone with the crease across the screen (tabletop) and the video takes the standing half, with the controls on the flat half. The YouTube player does the same, in its watch page and in fullscreen.

### Subtitles and audio tracks

- **Every kind of subtitle**: text subtitles (SRT, ASS, WebVTT, MP4 text) are drawn by Fathom in the size, colour, and background you set under **Settings → Audio & Subtitles**. Picture-based subtitles from discs (Blu-ray PGS, DVD VobSub, DVB) are drawn by the player itself, since they are images rather than text.
- **Subtitles beside the video**: a subtitle file stored next to the video on your server, including one a subtitle plugin downloaded, shows in the subtitle menu and loads when you pick it.
- **Tracks you can tell apart**: subtitle and audio tracks are named from what the server knows about them, for example **English (Forced, SRT)**, **English (Picture, PGS)**, **English (External, SRT)**, or **English (DTS-HD MA 5.1)**. Tracks that would otherwise look the same are numbered. A forced track only captions foreign-language dialogue, so it stays quiet during the rest.
- **Search Subtitles Online**: the last row of the subtitle menu searches the subtitle provider installed on your Jellyfin server (such as the OpenSubtitles plugin) and adds the one you pick straight to the video. Matches for your exact file come first. It searches your preferred subtitle language, or your device's language if you have not set one, and **Change Language** searches another. It needs a subtitle provider plugin on the server, and is shown to administrators and to accounts allowed to manage subtitles.

### Playback Info

Open the settings gear in the control bar and choose **Playback Info** for a read-only overlay of what is really happening, read live from mpv:

- **Play method**: direct play vs transcode (or, on YouTube, adaptive vs muxed).
- **Video and audio**: codecs, resolution, frame rate, bitrates.
- **Decoder**: whether hardware decoding actually engaged (for example `Hardware (vaapi)` vs `Software`), and dropped-frame counts.
- **General**: container, buffer, and A/V sync.

It works in both the Jellyfin and YouTube players, and stays put when you enter fullscreen.

## Offline downloads

Download a movie, an episode, a whole season or series, a Live TV recording, a track, or an entire album, and watch or listen with the server out of reach.

- **Downloads is a real library**, not a list of files, with its own place in the side menu: Movies, TV Shows, Recordings, and Music sections with the same artwork and rating badges as your online library.
- **Detail pages work offline** too, scoped to what you actually have: only the episodes on the device, with local Play, Mark Watched, and Remove that never touch the server.
- **Pick a scope** when downloading a series (everything, or a single season), and watch progress on a floating pill with a Cancel All action.
- **Choose where it lands** under **Settings → General**.

## Sleep timer

Every player has one, from the gear menu or the moon button in the top bar: music, video, YouTube, and radio. Pick anything from 15 minutes to 2 hours, or have it stop at the end of the current track or episode. The sound fades out over the last 15 seconds rather than cutting off, and the button shows the time left while it runs.

## Internet radio

Add stations by URL or search the built-in [radio-browser.info](https://www.radio-browser.info) directory. Organize them into groups and favorites, and reorder them by drag.

- **Live time-shift**: pause and rewind a live station within a buffered window, then hit **LIVE** to jump back to the edge.
- **Switch stations**: previous/next-station controls on the Now Playing screen, and on the OS media session too (the notification, Android Auto, and Linux/Windows media controls), so a keyboard media key or steering-wheel button skips to another saved station.
- **Now playing**: station art (with album-art lookup for stations that report track info), an inline volume control, and background playback.
- Radio can be cast to a speaker or TV alongside your music.

## Casting

Fathom casts to Chromecast and Google TV devices (on Android). Video direct-plays when the target supports the codecs, or falls back to a transcode for audio-only speakers, with a queue and skip that advance on the device.

## Android Auto

On Android, Fathom offers an audio-only Android Auto experience: browse your Jellyfin music, internet radio, and YouTube in tabs, search (including by voice) to play a song or playlist, and control playback, shuffle, repeat, favorites, and the up-next queue from the car's screen.

Because Fathom is sideloaded rather than installed from the Play Store, the first time you use it you need to allow apps from unknown sources in Android Auto's developer settings. See [Fathom does not appear in Android Auto](troubleshooting.md#fathom-does-not-appear-in-android-auto) if it is not listed.

## Android TV

Fathom on Android TV is driven with the remote alone.

- **Getting around**: Left goes to the side menu from anywhere and lands on the section you're in; Right comes back. A page opens with the remote on its first item, and what sits above it (a search box, **Filter**, **Sort**) is one press of Up away.
- **The remote's media buttons** work in every player: **Play**, **Pause**, **Rewind**, **Fast Forward** (not on live channels, which have nowhere to seek to), and **Next** and **Previous** for episodes. Holding Rewind or Fast Forward keeps stepping; the others act once per press.
- **Typing**: every text field, including the ones in **Administration** and **Profile**, opens the on-screen TV keyboard on Select. Its **→** key finishes: on the server address it connects, and on the password it signs in. A keyboard connected to the TV (Bluetooth or USB) types straight into it, and its Enter finishes too. The keyboard key beside the microphone switches to the TV's own keyboard for that field, which is the one a phone's remote app types into.
- **Reordering**: in **Home Layout** and **Navigation**, each row has **Move Up** and **Move Down**, since a remote can't drag.
- **Left to a phone or a computer**: picking several titles at once (**Select**), and the long-press menus on posters.

## Requests, ratings, and watch together

- **Seerr (optional)**: browse and request titles in the **Discover** section (long-press or right-click a poster for **Details** or **Request**), then approve, decline, or manage requests without leaving the page, with download progress shown right on the detail view. When **Search** finds nothing on your server for a movie or show, it offers **Search in Discover**, which opens Discover's Search with the same words, ready to request. With [Sonarr or Radarr](#sonarr-radarr-and-lidarr) connected too, the requests waiting for you are also listed in **Manage > Activity**, and a requested title opens straight in the app that's downloading it. Set it up in [First-run setup](setup.md#seerr-requests).
- **Ratings that matter to you**: Rotten Tomatoes, IMDb, and community scores, plus Letterboxd, Metacritic, Trakt, and others through MDBList.
- **Watch together**: SyncPlay-based shared playback that interoperates with the official Jellyfin apps and other clients, not only with other Fathom users.

## Sonarr, Radarr and Lidarr

With Sonarr, Radarr or Lidarr connected (see [First-run setup](setup.md#sonarr-radarr-and-lidarr)), the **Manage** section brings them into Fathom, in four tabs (**Library**, **Calendar**, **Activity** and **Wanted**):

- **Library**: your Radarr, Sonarr and Lidarr libraries as poster grids, with the same colored status bar the apps use. **Movies**, **Shows** and **Music** at the top switch between them; with only one app connected, its library is simply there. Search, filter (monitored, missing, downloaded, continuing, ended), and sort by title, date added, year, or size, **Ascending** or **Descending**. Music has the filters and sorts that fit artists: monitored, unmonitored and missing, by name, date added or size.
- **Collections** (the button beside Sort in the movie library; under **⋮** on a phone or in a narrow window): Radarr's film series, each with its movies in order, which ones you have, and how many are missing. Tap a movie you have to open it, or a missing one to add it. **Monitor** a collection and Radarr adds its missing movies that are already in cinemas or released (straight away, not at its next scheduled refresh) and the others once they are; the dialog says how many first and which settings they're added with. **Edit** changes those settings (quality profile, root folder, Minimum Availability, tags, and whether to search when a movie is added), the filter shows only collections with missing movies, and **Refresh Collections** updates them all.
- **Several at once**: tap **Select** beside Sort (under **⋮** on a phone or in a narrow window; or long-press a poster and pick **Select**), tap the titles you want or **Select All**, then act on them all from the bar above the grid, as in the apps' own select mode: **Edit** (monitored, quality profile, root folder with the choice to move the files, and Series Type, Season Folders and Monitor New Seasons for shows, Minimum Availability for movies, or Metadata Profile and Monitor New Albums for artists; every field starts on No Change), **Monitoring** for shows (which episodes, as on one show), **Tags** (add, remove, or replace), **Search**, **Refresh & Scan**, **Rename Files**, and **Delete**. Changing the search or filter drops titles that are no longer shown from the selection, so nothing out of sight is changed.
- **Calendar**: episodes airing, movie releases (in cinemas, digital, disc) and albums coming out, from every app connected, from yesterday through the next four weeks, grouped by day, each marked Downloaded, Missing, Unaired, or Not Released. It shows monitored titles; **Show Unmonitored** adds the rest, marked Unmonitored. On a tablet or a computer, or a phone held sideways, **Month** lays a whole month out as a grid, a square per day, colored the same way: the arrows move a month back or forward, **Today** comes back, and a day with more than fit in its square says how many more and opens the full list. Weeks start on the day they do in your device's region. Point at a release for its episode and time, or long-press it on a touchscreen. **Agenda** is the day-by-day list again. A phone held upright, any narrow window, and Android TV always show the list.
- **Activity**: three lists, **Queue**, **History** and **Blocklist**, and **Requests** as a fourth when Seerr is connected (see below). The tab counts the downloads in the queue, and the count turns orange when one of them needs help or when an app can't be reached. Each time you open the tab it starts on the queue.
- **Queue**: everything downloading in every app, with progress, time left, quality, and the download client. An album says which artist and album it is. Downloads that need help come first, with the app's own explanation: **Failed**, **Import Blocked**, **Import Failed** (Lidarr tried and couldn't), or **Needs Attention** when the app has a warning about one or can't reach the download client. It refreshes every few seconds while it's open. The **×** on a download removes it. You choose what happens in the download client: **Remove from Download Client** (with its data), **Change Category** (when the client moves finished downloads to a category of its own, such as qBittorrent's: it keeps the data, so it can go on seeding), or **Ignore Download** (it stays in the client as it is). You can also blocklist the release so it's never grabbed again, and search for another right away. A download held back by a delay profile has **Grab Now** to send it to the client straight away. **Select** picks several downloads to remove (or grab) together. Downloads the app couldn't match to a title are listed too, as on the apps' own queue, since they're the ones that need help.
- **Manual Import**: a finished download the app won't import on its own (**Import Blocked**, such as a release whose name doesn't match the show, or a file that isn't better than the one you have) has a **Manual Import** button, and so does a Lidarr download it tried and couldn't import (**Import Failed**). It lists the download's video or audio files with what the app thinks each one is and why it held back. Tap a file (or its pencil) to fix it: pick the **Show** and Sonarr works out the season and episodes from the file name, or choose the **Season** and **Episodes** yourself (two episodes for a double episode); pick the **Movie** for Radarr; pick the **Artist** and **Album** for Lidarr, which then works out the album's edition and which tracks each file is from its tags (or choose the **Tracks** yourself); and correct the **Quality**, **Languages**, or **Release Group**. The app checks the file again after each change. Tick the files to bring in and press **Import**: they go into the library as the app's own import would, and the download leaves the queue. With several ticked, **Edit** changes them all at once (useful for a season pack whose show wasn't recognized). The app's reasons are shown, but you can still import over them, as on the apps' own pages.
- **History**: everything the apps have done lately (grabs, imports, failed downloads, deletions, renames), newest first, with the release name, quality, and indexer or reason. Lidarr's say which album and track. A grab's **⋮** menu has **Mark as Failed**, which blocklists that release and, if the app is set to, searches for another. The filter shows one kind of event: Grabbed, Imported, Download Failed, File Deleted, File Renamed, or Download Ignored.
- **Blocklist**: the releases each app won't grab again, newest first, with the reason. **×** takes one off so it can be grabbed again, **Select** takes off several, and **Clear Blocklist** empties it.
- **Requests**: with [Seerr](setup.md#seerr-requests) connected and an account that can manage requests, the requests waiting for a yes or no, oldest first, with how many in the switch's label (a dot on a phone, where the number doesn't fit). **Approve** or **Decline** right on each one, as in the **Discover** section, with **Edit Request** and **Delete Request** under its **⋮** menu (or a right-click, or a long press); Seerr sends an approved request on to Sonarr or Radarr, and its download shows in the queue once it starts. The first 100 are listed, and **Load More** at the end brings the next 100. Accounts that can't approve requests don't see the list.
- **Wanted**: monitored episodes, movies and albums that are **Missing**, or whose file is below the quality profile's cutoff (**Cutoff Unmet**, with the quality you have now for episodes), newest first. The search button on each searches for that one, and **Search All** searches for everything on the list. The filter switches to unmonitored titles instead, and **Select** picks several to search for, or to monitor or stop monitoring together. Radarr also lists monitored movies that aren't out yet; they're marked Not Released and come last.
- **Manual Import from a folder**: the folder button on **Wanted** (or **More > Manual Import** on a movie, show or artist, for files in its own folder that the app hasn't picked up) imports files that never came through the queue. Browse the server's folders or type a path; your last few folders are kept. Choose **Move Files** or **Copy Files** first (copies use hard links where the disk allows, so they take no extra space). Files already in your library are left out.
- **Detail pages**: a movie's or show's quality profile, path, size, release dates, every season and episode with its file and quality, the file's video, audio, and subtitle details, and its history.

What you can do from a movie or show:

- **Watch**: when the title is in your Jellyfin library, **Watch** is the page's main button and opens it there, ready to play. Fathom finds it by its TMDB, TVDB or IMDb id, so a downloaded title that Jellyfin has under another name, or in another language, is still found. Until the title is in Jellyfin, the search button is the main one: after a download, **Watch** shows once Jellyfin has scanned the new file in. Fathom looks again by itself for the first few minutes, and whenever you refresh the page.
- **Search**: **Search Movie**, or **Search Monitored** for a show, has the app search your indexers and grab the best release it finds. Each season has its own search button, and so does each episode (tap the episode). A season's **⋮** menu has its **Interactive Search**.
- **Interactive Search**: lists every release your indexers offer for a movie, a season, or an episode, the way the apps' own Interactive Search does: quality, size, age, indexer, Usenet or torrent (with seeders and leechers), custom format score, and the app's verdict. Releases the app rejected say why, and **Grab** still lets you take one after confirming. Sort by Best Match (the app's own ranking), Quality, Size, Age, or Seeders, and hide rejected releases if you like.
- **Monitor** or **Unmonitor** a movie, a season (its bookmark), or an episode.
- **Monitoring**, the bookmark on a show, does what Sonarr's monitoring options do: switch the whole show on or off, set which episodes it looks for in one go (**All Episodes**, **Future Episodes**, **Missing Episodes**, **Existing Episodes**, **Recent Episodes**, **Pilot Episode**, **First Season**, **Last Season**, or **None**, each explained as you pick it), whether to include the specials, and whether seasons announced later are monitored automatically (**Monitor New Seasons**).
- **Edit**: the quality profile, the **Path** (change it and you're asked whether to move the files there), and **Tags** (pick existing ones or make a **New Tag**), plus monitoring and Minimum Availability for a movie, or Series Type and Season Folders for a show.
- **Delete files in bulk**: a season's **⋮** menu has **Delete Season Files**, and **More > Manage Files** on a show lists every file by season, so you can tick whole seasons or single episodes and delete them together. Both say how many files and how much space first, and offer to unmonitor those episodes too (on by default), so the app doesn't just download them again.
- **Fix a file**: in **Manage Files**, a file's pencil opens **Edit File**: move it to different episodes (when the app matched it wrongly), or correct its quality, languages, or release group, which is what the app compares against your quality profile when deciding on upgrades. Tick several and **Edit** sets the quality, languages, or release group for all of them. A movie's file has **Edit File** too.
- Under **More**: **Refresh & Scan**, **Rename Files** (shows each file's current and new name first; it needs renaming turned on in the app's Settings, Media Management), **Open in Sonarr** or **Open in Radarr**, and **Delete**, with the choice to delete the files too and to keep import lists from adding it again.
- **Delete File** under a file removes just that file from disk, and **Edit File** beside it corrects its quality, languages, or release group.

**Music, with Lidarr**: an artist's page works the way a show's does, with its albums in place of seasons.

- **The page**: the artist's quality and metadata profiles, whether it's monitored and which new albums are, how many of the wanted tracks you have, its path, size and history. Its albums are listed newest first, under **Albums**, **EPs**, **Singles** and the rest when it has more than one kind, each with its cover, year, how many of its tracks you have, and the same colored bar as a season.
- **An album** opens into its tracks, numbered as the release numbers them (A1, B2 on vinyl), with each disc under its own heading on a release of several. Each track says **Missing**, **Not Released**, **Unmonitored**, or the quality of its file. Tap a track for its file: the path, quality, size, and the audio's format, bit depth and sample rate, with **Delete File**.
- **Play**: when the artist is in your Jellyfin library, **Play** opens it there. Fathom finds it by its MusicBrainz id, which Jellyfin reads from your files' tags.
- **Search**: **Search Monitored** has Lidarr look for every monitored album, and each album has its own search button. An album's **⋮** menu has its **Interactive Search**, and **Delete Album Files**, which deletes its track files from disk (Lidarr keeps the album).
- **Monitoring**, the bookmark on an artist, does what Lidarr's monitoring options do: switch the artist on or off, set which albums it looks for in one go (**All Albums**, **Future Albums**, **Missing Albums**, **Existing Albums**, **Latest Album**, **First Album** or **None**, each explained as you pick it), and which albums found later are monitored (**Monitor New Albums**: all, only new releases, or none). An album's own bookmark switches it alone.
- **Edit**: the quality profile, the **Metadata Profile** (which kinds of release Lidarr keeps of the artist), the path, and tags. Under **More**: **Refresh & Scan**, **Rename Files**, **Open in Lidarr**, and **Delete**.

Lidarr's albums are in the Calendar, the Activity lists, Wanted, Health and the Home rows, each searched for, monitored and picked as an album.

On a phone, or any narrow window, the page keeps the search button and the bookmark, and **Interactive Search** and **Edit** move under **More** with the rest. The lists do the same with their own buttons: on **Wanted**, for example, **Search All**, **Manual Import** and **Select** are under **⋮**.

**On a tablet held sideways or in a computer window**, when there's room, a title opens in a pane beside the list instead of on a page of its own. The list stays where it was, with the open title marked, so you can go down it one title after another. Everything the page does works in the pane. The arrows at its top right open the full page, and **×**, **Esc** or Back closes it. If the window gets too narrow for it, the pane goes away, and comes back with the same title once there's room again. In a wide window the lists keep to a width that reads well instead of stretching across it.

**Right-click** a poster, a download, a release on the Calendar, or a row in Wanted, History or Blocklist (long-press on a touchscreen) for what you can do with it without opening it: **Open**, **Open Full Page** when titles open in the pane, and the row's own actions, such as **Search Movie**, **Interactive Search**, **Mark as Failed**, **Remove from Blocklist**, or **Select**.

**Keyboard**, on a computer: **Ctrl+1** to **Ctrl+4** switch tabs, **Ctrl+F** searches the library, **F5** or **Ctrl+R** refreshes the list, or the movie or show that's open, and **Esc** leaves the search box, then closes the pane, then ends Select.

**On Android TV**, the remote covers the day to day: the Calendar, the queue and the other Activity lists, Wanted, browsing the library and Collections, a movie's, show's or artist's page, Prowlarr's indexers (on, off and tested) and its search, the download clients (pausing, removing, Move to Top and the speed limits), searching (automatic and **Interactive Search**), adding, monitoring, **Edit** (quality profile, tags and the rest, apart from the path), and taking a download off the queue or sending a held one with **Grab Now**. A page opens on its first item rather than on the buttons above it, which stay one press of Up away, and **Add** opens on its search box. In a list, a row with buttons of its own (a download's **×**, a release's **Grab**, a season's or album's search and bookmark) is one stop going up and down, and Right steps onto its buttons. On a title's page, what's there to read (the facts, the overview, a file's details, the history) is a stop too, so Down goes through all of it, and Up from the top brings the header's buttons back. A download the app couldn't match opens its menu when you press it. Left goes to the side menu from anywhere, and Right comes back to where you were. **Manual Import**, **Import Library**, **Select**, **Manage Files**, **Delete File**, **Delete Season Files** and **Delete Album Files**, **Rename Files**, changing a title's path, and **Open in Sonarr**, **Open in Radarr** or **Open in Lidarr** are left to a phone or a computer. Menus with a tick beside the choice in use (**Sort**, **Filter**) are picked with Select like any other. **Settings → Connections** works with the remote too: a connection's page opens on its first empty field, and addresses and keys go in with the TV keyboard.

**Add**, at the top of the Manage section, finds new movies, shows and artists online. From the Library it looks for the kind you have open, movies, shows or music; from the other tabs it searches every app at once, with **All**, **Movies**, **Shows** and **Music** to narrow it down. Search by name, or exactly by id: `imdb:` asks Radarr and Sonarr, `tmdb:` looks up movies, `tvdb:` looks up shows, and `lidarr:` with an artist's MusicBrainz id looks up artists. Artists of the same name are told apart by what kind of artist they are and how MusicBrainz describes them. Searching your library also offers **Add "…"** to look up what you typed. Titles the app already has say **In Library** and open their page.

Adding asks for the root folder (with its free space), the quality profile, what to monitor, tags, and Minimum Availability for a movie, Series Type, Season Folders and Monitor New Seasons for a show, or the **Metadata Profile**, which albums to monitor and **Monitor New Albums** for an artist, and can start a search right away (for an artist, for its missing albums; for a show, for missing episodes, for better copies of episodes below the quality cutoff, or both). It starts on your saved defaults, or, until you have some, on what most of your library already uses (the quality profile says which), so a new title gets the same quality as the rest. What you pick is saved as the starting point for the next one, the way the apps' own web pages work, and you can set it yourself with **Defaults for New Titles** on the app's page in Settings > Connections. For an artist, what most of your artists use is the starting point in the same way, after what the root folder is set up with in Lidarr (its quality and metadata profiles, which albums to monitor, and tags), as Lidarr's own Add does; picking another root folder brings its defaults.

If the title is already on the server's disk in a folder the app doesn't manage, such as a movie Jellyfin plays that was never added to Radarr, its search result says **On Disk** and Add shows **Already on Disk** with the folder. Adding uses that folder and the files in it instead of making a new one, so nothing downloads twice, and starting a search is left unticked. Turn the switch off to have the app make its own folder instead.

**Import Library** (the button beside Sort in the Library; under **⋮** on a phone or in a narrow window) adds shows or movies that are already on disk, all at once, as the apps' own Library Import does. It lists every folder in your root folders that the app doesn't manage yet and matches each one to a title by its folder name. A folder whose title the app already has says so, one with no match has **Find** to search by another name, and **Change** fixes a wrong match. Choose how they're added under **Add With** (quality profile, what to monitor, and Series Type and Season Folders for shows or Minimum Availability for movies), tick the folders, and **Import**: each title keeps its folder and the files already in it. Nothing is searched for unless you tick the search option.

**Indexers**, a button at the top of the Manage section (under **More** on a phone) when [Prowlarr](setup.md#sonarr-radarr-and-lidarr) is connected, brings in the indexer manager behind the apps:

- **Indexers**: each indexer with its kind (Usenet or torrent), privacy, priority and the kinds of release it has, a switch to turn it on or off, and **Test**. One that Prowlarr is holding back after failures says so, and when it tries it again. **Test All** tests every indexer that's on and marks each, and **Sync App Indexers** hands them to Sonarr, Radarr and Lidarr again. Indexers are added in Prowlarr itself.
- **Search**: searches every indexer that's on at once, for everything or for movies, shows, music or books, sorted by newest, size or seeders. **Grab** sends a release to Prowlarr's own download client.
- **Stats**: how many searches and grabs each indexer has answered, how many failed, and how quickly it answers.
- **History**: Prowlarr's searches, RSS checks and grabs, newest first, with the indexer, what asked, and how many results.

**Subtitles**, a button at the top of the Manage section (under **More** on a phone) when [Bazarr](setup.md#sonarr-radarr-and-lidarr) is connected:

- **Wanted**: the episodes and movies missing subtitles, and in which languages (HI for the hard of hearing, Forced for foreign-language lines). **Search** has Bazarr find the best for each missing language and keep it; **Manual Search** lists every provider's subtitles for it, scored by how well they match the file, to pick one and **Download** it; **Search All** searches for everything missing. Bazarr does these a moment later: the row says Searching meanwhile, and the list is asked again a few seconds on.
- **History**: what Bazarr did, newest first: downloaded, downloaded by hand, upgraded, uploaded, synced, translated, extracted from the video, or deleted, with the language, the provider, the score and when.
- **Providers**: each subtitle provider Bazarr asks and whether it's working, or what went wrong and when Bazarr tries it again; **Try All Again** lets it try every one now.

Bazarr's health problems show in **Health** with the apps'.

**Download Clients**, a button at the top of the Manage section (under **More** on a phone) when [qBittorrent or SABnzbd](setup.md#sonarr-radarr-and-lidarr) is connected, shows what each client is doing, asked again every few seconds while it's open:

- **Each client**: its speed (down, and up for qBittorrent), its free space, and whether qBittorrent is firewalled or disconnected. **Pause All** or **Resume All** for the whole client, qBittorrent's **Alternative Speed Limits** (the limits set in qBittorrent itself), and SABnzbd's **Speed Limit**, from 1 to 50 MB/s.
- **Each download**: its progress, size, speed, time left and category, and for a torrent its seeds and peers, or its ratio once it's done. Keep the list to **Active**, **Paused**, **Finished** or **Failed** downloads. Pause or resume one, move it to the top of SABnzbd's queue with **Move to Top**, or **Remove** it, with its files if you like.
- **History**, with SABnzbd: finished and failed jobs, why one failed, **Retry**, and **Remove**.
- Pressing a download, a client or a finished job (or a right-click, or a long press) lists what can be done with it by name.
- **Add Download** (the link button) takes a magnet link, or a link to a .torrent or an NZB, sends it to the client the link suits (or the one you pick), and puts it in a category.

**Switch Server**, at the top of the Manage section once an app has [more than one server](setup.md#sonarr-radarr-and-lidarr) (a 4K Radarr, say), picks the one in use; every page then shows that server: its library, calendar, queue, wanted list and health.

**Notifications**: Fathom tells you what Sonarr, Radarr and Lidarr did (a download brought in, a download that failed and why, and new health problems; each grab too, if you turn that on), in the bell and, on a desktop, as a system notification while Fathom isn't in front. A season imported at once is one notification. Choose which in **Settings → General → Notifications**, along with how often to check while Fathom isn't showing (every minute while it shows). There's no server in between: Fathom asks the apps itself. On Android it also does while Fathom is closed, as system notifications, at most every 15 minutes (Android's shortest) and only with a network; on a desktop, only while Fathom runs, catching up on what happened when it opens again (after a long time away, the newest ten and how many more). With [more than one server](setup.md#sonarr-radarr-and-lidarr) of an app, it watches each, and says which ("Downloaded · 4K"); one from a server not in use opens the Manage section, as its titles are numbered differently.

**Health**, the shield at the top of the Manage section, shows each app's version (Prowlarr's too) and what its own health check has found, such as an indexer that keeps failing or a missing root folder, with **Learn More** linking to the app's wiki page about it. A badge counts the warnings and errors.

**From a Jellyfin movie or show**: when Radarr (movies) or Sonarr (shows) is connected, the **⋮** menu on the title's page finds it there by its TMDB or TVDB id. If the app has it, **Manage in Radarr** or **Manage in Sonarr** opens its page in the Manage section, and **Search Movie** (or **Search Monitored**, for missing episodes) and, for a movie, **Interactive Search**, for a better copy, start right there. If the app doesn't have it, **Add to Radarr** or **Add to Sonarr** opens straight into adding that title, using its folder on disk when the app can see it. Jellyfin titles without the id don't show these.

**From a Jellyfin artist**: when Lidarr is connected, an artist's page in your music library has **Manage in Lidarr** at the top when Lidarr has the artist, or **Add to Lidarr** when it doesn't, which opens Add on that artist. Fathom finds it by the MusicBrainz id Jellyfin read from your files' tags, so an artist without one doesn't show either.

**From a Seerr request**: when Fathom's Radarr or Sonarr has a title you're looking at in **Discover**, its page has a **Manage in Radarr** or **Manage in Sonarr** button, and so does each request in the request lists (under **⋮** on a phone, and while the request still waits for an answer or a retry). It opens the title's page in the Manage section, where you can see the download, search again, or pick another release. The download progress on a Seerr title's page opens it too. The button shows only when the same title is in the app you connected to Fathom.

**On Home**: two rows, both of which you can move or turn off in Settings > Home > **Home Layout**.

- **Downloading Now**: a card for every movie, show and artist in the queue, with how far along it is and the time left, or a word in its place when it's doing something else (queued, paused, importing, or needing help, the last in orange or red). A show with several episodes downloading is one card that counts them, and an artist says the album, or how many albums. The row keeps itself up to date while Home is open. Tap a card for the title's page, or **See All** for the queue.
- **Coming This Week**: monitored episodes, movies and albums due today or in the next six days that you don't have yet, soonest first, each marked with its day (**Today**, **Tomorrow**, or the weekday). A show with more than one episode due, or an artist with more than one album, says how many more. Tap a poster for the title's page, or **See All** for the Calendar.

A row with nothing to show isn't there at all, so Home stays as it was on a quiet day, or when the apps can't be reached. Both rows are on Android TV too.

The apps' one-time setup (indexers, download clients, custom formats, and media management rules) stays in their own web pages.

## Server administration

Run most of Jellyfin from the app, without opening the web dashboard: manage users, libraries, scheduled tasks, active sessions, playback and transcoding, networking, branding, Live TV and DVR, backups, and plugins. Each plugin's configuration is an in-app form (toggles, fields, and add/remove lists), with a raw-JSON option if you prefer, and plugin logos show in the list.

**Backups** (on servers that offer them): create a backup of the server, choosing whether to include metadata, subtitles, and trickplay images alongside the database, see what each backup holds and the server version it was made on, and restore one. Backups are stored on the server itself. Restoring restarts the server and replaces everything changed since the backup was made, and should only be done onto the same Jellyfin version the backup came from.

## Personalization

Light, dark, and AMOLED themes with a custom accent color, an Interface Size setting for high-resolution screens (desktop), a Home layout you can rearrange, a sidebar whose items you can reorder or hide (Libraries, Browse and Downloads each on their own), in-app updates with a Stable or Dev channel, system and in-app notifications, a Settings screen you can search, and a fully translatable interface. In a wide window, Settings and its pages keep to a centered column so a setting's name and its switch stay close together.

- **Several accounts**: keep more than one Jellyfin account (or server) signed in and switch between them without re-entering anything.
- **Screen readers**: cards announce themselves as a single item with their title and year, every icon-only button carries a label, and the Movie Night Wheel names its titles and reads out each result.
- **Reduced motion**: with the device's Reduce Motion (or Remove Animations) setting on, pages fade in without sliding, and what moves by itself (the playing bars, the recording dot, the sync pulse) holds still.
- **Colors that read**: status colors are deepened in the light theme, so **Connected**, **Available** and **Pending** stay legible on a light page.
- **When something fails**: a page that can't load says why in plain words, with **Retry**. For anything unexpected, turn on **Diagnostic Logging** in Settings to capture the detail for a bug report.

## Notifications

Fathom keeps an in-app notification centre (the bell), and shows an in-app toast while you're using the app. Choose which events you care about under **Settings → General → Notifications**:

- **Seerr requests**: a new request (pending approval), or one of yours being approved, declined, or becoming available.
- **Download complete**: when a download to this device finishes.
- **Update available**: when a new version of Fathom is released.
- **Sonarr, Radarr and Lidarr**: **Downloaded**, **Download Failed**, **Grabbed** and **Health Problems**, with **Check the Manage Apps** for how often Fathom asks them; see [Notifications in the Manage section](#sonarr-radarr-and-lidarr).

While the app is open, Fathom checks Seerr for status changes every minute or so, so updates surface almost immediately as a toast and land in the bell. On **desktop**, when Fathom is in the background these also appear as system (OS) pop-ups, toggled by the master switch at the top of the section. On **mobile**, backgrounded events wait quietly in the bell rather than raising a system notification, except an available update and, on Android, what Sonarr, Radarr and Lidarr did while Fathom was closed. Either way, the per-event toggles decide what gets recorded in the first place.
